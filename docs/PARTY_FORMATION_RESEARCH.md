# Party-formation research baseline — WORKING, not legal advice

Reviewed: 2026-07-17

This baseline uses current official federal sources and intentionally avoids a universal “start a party” checklist. State ballot and party-recognition law varies and must be researched jurisdiction by jurisdiction before action.

## Distinct status questions

1. **Civic or political association:** creating a name, website, working group, or bylaws does not by itself establish ballot or federal party status.
2. **State ballot access and party recognition:** the Federal Election Commission states that ballot access is governed by state law and directs organizations to the relevant Secretary of State or equivalent election office. Requirements may include petitions, candidate filings, voter-support thresholds, deadlines, and vote-retention rules.
3. **Federal political committee registration:** the FEC explains that state or national party organizations generally trigger federal registration after raising more than $1,000 or spending more than $1,000 in contributions or other expenditures during a calendar year. Registration uses Form 1 and creates recurring reporting duties. Exact classification and activity matter.
4. **Qualified political-party committee status:** the FEC distinguishes merely registering a committee from qualifying for party benefits. Federal political-party status depends on party-designated federal candidates appearing on ballots; state and national committee status has additional criteria. The FEC recommends seeking an advisory opinion before relying on expanded national-committee limits.
5. **Tax treatment:** IRS Section 527 notice and reporting rules contain exceptions, thresholds, deadlines, and public-disclosure consequences. Federal political committees required to report under federal election law may be treated differently from other political organizations. Tax analysis must be coordinated with committee and state-law analysis.

## Pre-activation research sequence

`DAN NEEDED` decisions precede legal implementation: choose the intended initial activity, organizational scope, leadership, responsible officers, states, candidate horizon, and whether any funds or in-kind support will be accepted. Then retain appropriately qualified campaign-finance, state-election, organizational, tax, privacy, and trademark counsel. Build a dated jurisdiction matrix from each state’s primary authorities. Do not accept money, incur regulated spending, nominate candidates, or claim status based on this planning summary.
